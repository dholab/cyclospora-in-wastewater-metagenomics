# Citation source locations

For every factual claim in the [README](README.md) Introduction and Discussion, this document records the exact
place in the primary source where that information is found, together with the source's own words.
The purpose is to make each statement checkable without re-reading the whole paper.

All bibliographic records were retrieved from **NCBI PubMed**. DOI links are given for every
reference.

## How to read this document

- **Claim** is the assertion as it appears in the README, condensed.
- **Location** is the section, paragraph, table, or figure of the source.
- **Source text** is quoted verbatim from the source.

### Two caveats about the quoted text

1. **Stripped organism names.** The machine-readable full text supplied by PubMed Central omits
   text that is italicized in the published article, which for these papers is almost always the
   organism name. Quotes below therefore contain gaps where *Cyclospora* or *C. cayetanensis*
   stood. Where the missing word is unambiguous from context it is restored in square brackets, for
   example `[*Cyclospora*] oocysts are hardy`. Nothing else has been altered, and no gap has been
   filled where the missing word was not certain.
2. **Verification level.** Not every source could be read in full. Each reference below is labeled
   with what was actually consulted — full text, abstract, or bibliographic metadata alone. Claims
   are only made in the README to the depth the corresponding source was verified.

> **Note on the two species names.** The two `sp. nov.` names are italicized in the published
> abstract and are therefore absent from the machine-retrieved abstract text (shown as `[*…*]`
> above). They are recovered from the article's own author keyword list, which contains exactly
> three *Cyclospora* binomials: *C. ashfordi*, *C. cayetanensis*, and *C. henanensis*. Since
> *C. cayetanensis* is the redescribed species, the two novel species are *C. ashfordi* and
> *C. henanensis*. *C. ashfordi* is independently confirmed as a human-infecting species by
> [Shen et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.3201/eid3102.240399), which reports epidemiologic
> associations for it by name. *C. henanensis* is consistent with strain CHN_HEN01 (Henan, China)
> named in the abstract as representing the distinct lineage C.
